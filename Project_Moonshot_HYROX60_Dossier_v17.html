Running Engine Thesis

Accumulation wins
8 km
Race running volume

HYROX is not a lifting event with some jogging. It is eight separate 1 km runs with stations interrupting rhythm, breathing, and leg turnover.

100+
Monthly mile floor

The goal is not one heroic run. The goal is repeatable accumulation: steady miles, controlled fatigue, and enough durability to keep running late in the race.

13K-20K
Monthly vertical target

Elevation is the edge-builder: leg durability, cardiac output, mental toughness, and fatigue resistance. It supports HYROX, but it does not replace flat compromised 1 km practice.

What we learned: HYROX looks like a lifting race, but the runners usually decide the ceiling. Strength keeps the stations from becoming disasters; running capacity keeps the whole race from bleeding minutes. The plan is to become strong enough to survive the stations and fit enough to keep running when others start walking.

  • No hero simulations yet.

  • Run-station-run bricks.
  • Race-weight sled confidence.
  • Wall ball volume and legal-rep consistency.
  • Ryan mileage checkpoint.

Simulation

  • Partial HYROX efforts.
  • Team station splits.
  • Transition choreography.
  • Full-style effort by Week 10.
Weeks 11-12

Sharpen + Taper

  • Reduce volume.
  • Keep intensity.
  • No proving fitness.
  • Arrive fresh.

Weekly Command Report

Mission control format

1. Team Scoreboard

  • Week dates.
  • Vudi: miles, elevation, long run, total training hours.
  • Ryan: miles, long run, total training hours.
  • Team: combined miles, HYROX station sessions, lift sessions, simulations.
  • Recovery: RHR, HRV, sleep average, soreness / pain status.
  • Optional attachments: Strava screenshots, weekly summary, activity links, row/ski monitor photos.

2. What Got Done

  • Running accumulation: total miles, elevation, long run, consistency.
  • Station work: ski, row, sled push, sled pull, wall balls, burpees, lunges, carries.
  • Strength signal: bench / squat / deadlift maintained or not maintained.
  • Quality signal: what felt smooth, what broke down, what needs coaching.

3. Decision Checkpoint

  • Only log strategic choices, not every workout.
  • Example: "Keep 30-35 mile weeks while adding one HYROX brick."
  • Why: build engine without sacrificing strength.
  • Expected gain: better compromised running.
  • Expected risk: recovery interference.
  • Observed result: pending / positive / negative.

4. Next Orders

  • Increase: one measurable input.
  • Protect: one thing that must not decline.
  • Stop/reduce: one wasteful or risky behavior.
  • Red flag: one honest concern.
  • Next target: one number to hit next week.

This is not paperwork. It is mission control: totals, evidence, decisions, next orders. Less room for error. More room for honesty.

Nutrition + Recovery

Support the workload

Fuel

Do not under-eat. Protein stays high, carbs support runs and HYROX sessions, and electrolytes become non-negotiable during heat and double days.

Performance Supports

Creatine stays. Beet powder and pomegranate juice can be daily if tolerated. They support nitric oxide and blood flow, but they do not replace food or sleep.

Recovery

Sleep is the hidden limiter. More mileage plus heavy lifting only works if HRV, RHR, mood, and joints stay stable.

Principles

Non-negotiables

Not bench. Not deadlift. Not squat. Not even 5K. 1 km after a sled push.

Run 1 km. Push the sled. Run another 1 km. The difference tells us whether strength is helping or fatigue is stealing the race.

Running first. Strength maintenance second.

Both athletes are strong enough. The team gets fastest when the running floor rises and station execution becomes automatic.
